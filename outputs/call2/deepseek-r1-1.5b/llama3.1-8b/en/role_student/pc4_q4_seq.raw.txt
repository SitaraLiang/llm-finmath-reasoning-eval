### Question block 2
## Step 1: Recognize that $W_t$ is a martingale
$W_t$ is a Brownian motion, which is known to be a martingale.

## Step 2: Apply Ito's lemma on the function $f(t) = t^2$
For a martingale $M_t$, Ito's lemma gives:
$d(M_t^2) = 2 M_t dM_t + (dM_t)^2$

## Step 3: Apply Ito's lemma to the Brownian motion $W_t$
Applying this to $W_t$ gives:
$d((W_t)^2) = 2 W_t dW_t + (dW_t)^2$

## Step 4: Integrate both sides from 0 to $\tau$
Integrating both sides from 0 to $\tau$ gives:
$(W_{\tau})^2 - (W_0)^2 = \int_0^\tau 2 W_s dW_s + \int_0^\tau (dW_s)^2$

## Step 5: Take expectation on both sides
Taking expectation on both sides, noting that $W_0 = 0$, gives:
$\mathbb{E}[(W_{\tau})^2] = \mathbb{E}\left[\int_0^\tau (dW_s)^2\right]$

## Step 6: Evaluate the expectation of the integral
Since each increment of Brownian motion has variance $s$, we have:
$\mathbb{E}\left[\int_0^\tau (dW_s)^2\right] = \int_0^\tau s ds = \frac{\tau^2}{2}$

## Step 7: Conclude the expected value of $(W_{\tau})^2$
Thus, the expected value of $(W_{\tau})^2$ is $\boxed{\dfrac{\tau^2}{2}}$

The final answer is: $\boxed{\dfrac{\tau^2}{2}}$
