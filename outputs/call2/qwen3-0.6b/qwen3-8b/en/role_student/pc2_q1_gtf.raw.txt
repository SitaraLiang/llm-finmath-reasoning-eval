To determine the final answer, we analyze the process $ X_t = t W_t $, where $ W_t $ is a standard Wiener process (Brownian motion). The key property of a Wiener process is that its variance at time $ t $ is:

$$
\text{Var}(W_t) = t
$$

Now, consider the process $ X_t = t W_t $. The variance of $ X_t $ is:

$$
\text{Var}(X_t) = \text{Var}(t W_t) = t^2 \cdot \text{Var}(W_t) = t^2 \cdot t = t^3
$$

If we are specifically interested in the variance at time $ t = 1 $, then:

$$
\text{Var}(X_1) = 1^3 = 1
$$

Thus, the final answer is:

$$
\boxed{1}
