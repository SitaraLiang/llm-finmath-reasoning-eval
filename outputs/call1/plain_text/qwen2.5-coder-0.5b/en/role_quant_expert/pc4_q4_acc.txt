Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
To compute the expected value of a Brownian motion $W_t$, we use the following formula:

\[
\mathbb{E}(W_t) = \frac{\sigma^2}{2} T \int_{0}^{t} W_s \, ds + c \quad \text{for bounded stopping time}
\]

where:
- $\sigma$ is the drift rate of the Brownian motion,
- $T$ is the stopping time,
- $c$ is a constant that does not depend on $W_t$, but is often set to zero for simplicity.

This formula accounts for both continuous and discrete processes. It shows how the expected value changes with respect to time, allowing us to evaluate the process at different points over the given stopping period without explicitly integrating it in the numerator or denominator.
