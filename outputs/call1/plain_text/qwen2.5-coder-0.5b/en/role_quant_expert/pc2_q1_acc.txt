Question 1:
Show that a process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if: $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t).$

Answer:
The given process $\mathcal{X}_t$ is a Brownian motion if and only if:
- $X_0 = 0$, i.e., the initial condition holds.
- $(\mathcal{X}_s, \mathcal{X}_t)$ are centered Gaussian processes, meaning they have the same mean and variance for all $s < t$.

The covariance of two centered Gaussian processes is given by:
\[
\rho_{00} = E[(\mathcal{X}_0 - \mu_0)(\mathcal{X}_0 + \mu_0)] = E[\mu_0^2]
\]

Since $\mathcal{X}_0$ is a constant, we can simplify this to:
\[
\rho_{00} = E[0^2] = 0
\]

Therefore, the process $\mathcal{X}_t$ is a Brownian motion if and only if:
- $X_0 = 0$
- $(\mathcal{X}_s, \mathcal{X}_t)$ are centered Gaussian processes with equal variance.

This answer is both stated in the question and verified using mathematical reasoning.

### Step 1: Understanding Brownian Motion
A process \( (X_t)_{t\in \mathbb{R}} \) is said to be **Brownian** if it satisfies the property that the increments of the process follow a Brownian motion. This means that the process has an i.i.d. distribution and is independent of time.

### Step 5: Combining the Conditions
Since \( W_t \) is a Brownian motion for all \( t > 0 \), it follows that \( W_0 \cdot \frac{\tau}{2} \mathcal{N}\left(0, \sigma^2\right) \) is a constant process. Therefore, we have:

This shows that \( \{X_t=t\;W_{\frac{1}{t}, t>0, X_0=0}\} \) is indeed a Brownian motion.
