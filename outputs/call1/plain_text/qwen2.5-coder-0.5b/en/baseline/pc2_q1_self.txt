Question 1:
Show that a process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if: $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t).$

Answer:

1. **Central Gaussian Process (CGP):**
   - A center-gaussian process has the form $X = \mu + \sigma Z$, where $\mu$ is the mean and $\sigma$ is the covariance matrix.
   - The covariance matrix of a centered Gaussian process can be expressed as:
     \[
     \sigma^2 = \frac{1}{\text{variance}}
     \]
   - Therefore, if $X_t = \mu + \sigma Z$, then $Z$ follows a standard normal distribution. This implies that $\text{cov}(Z, Z) = 0$. Hence, the process is a centered Gaussian process.

2. **Brownian Motion (Brownian Motion):**
   - A Brownian motion is defined as an almost continuous process where each step has a constant speed.
   - For $\{X_t\}_{t\in \mathbb{R}^+\}$ to be a brownian motion, there must exist a time $T > 0$ such that the process satisfies $E[X_T] = 0$ and $E[X_{T+1}] = X_T + Z$. Here, $Z$ follows a standard normal distribution. This implies that $\text{cov}(X_S, X_T) = \sigma^2$, which is consistent with the definition of Brownian motion.

Since both conditions (being a centered Gaussian process and being a brownian motion) are satisfied by the given process $\{X_t\}_{t\in \mathbb{R}^+\}$, the process satisfies both properties.

Therefore, the answer to Question 1 is:
