Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
The expected value of a Brownian motion $W_t$, denoted by $\E(W_t)$, is equal to the average of its increments over a given interval, $\Delta t$. The standard deviation of a Brownian motion is the same as its mean, $\mu$, and it is denoted by $\sigma$.

Given:
- $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time (i.e., $0 < \tau < T$).

Using the formula for the expected value of a Brownian motion with an interval,
\[
E(W_t) = \frac{1}{2} \Delta t \mu + W_0
\]
where:
- $\Delta t$ is the time interval over which the increment is calculated.
- $W_0$ is the initial condition, which represents the value of the Brownian motion at time 0.

Thus,
\[
E(W_\tau) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot T \mu + W_0
\]

This formula provides a concise and direct way to compute the expected value of the Brownian motion over a bounded stopping time.
