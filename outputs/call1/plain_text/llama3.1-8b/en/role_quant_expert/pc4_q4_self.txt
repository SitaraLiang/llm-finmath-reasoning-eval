Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the definition of Brownian Motion
A Brownian Motion $W_t$ satisfies the following properties:
- $W_0 = 0$
- $W_t - W_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t-s)$ for $t > s$
- The increments are independent

## Step 2: Recall the definition of expectation
The expectation of a random variable $X$ is defined as $\E(X) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x f(x) dx$, where $f(x)$ is the probability density function of $X$.

## Step 3: Compute the expectation of $W_\tau$
Since $W_t$ is a Brownian Motion, we have that $\E(W_\tau) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x f_{\tau}(x) dx$, where $f_{\tau}(x)$ is the probability density function of $W_\tau$. However, since $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, we can use the fact that $W_\tau \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \E(\tau))$.

## Step 4: Use the properties of Brownian Motion
Since $W_t$ is a Brownian Motion, we have that $\E(W_\tau) = \E(\E(W_\tau | \mathcal{F}_\tau))$, where $\mathcal{F}_\tau$ is the filtration generated by $\tau$. Using the law of iterated expectations, we get $\E(W_\tau) = \E(W_\tau)$.

## Step 5: Simplify the expression
Since $W_t$ is a Brownian Motion, we have that $\E(W_\tau) = 0$.

The final answer is: $\boxed{0}$
