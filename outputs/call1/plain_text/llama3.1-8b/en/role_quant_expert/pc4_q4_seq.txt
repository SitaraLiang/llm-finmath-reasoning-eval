Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the definition of a Brownian Motion
A Brownian Motion $W_t$ satisfies the following properties:
- $W_0 = 0$
- $W_t - W_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t-s)$ for $t > s$
- The increments are independent

## Step 2: Recall the definition of a bounded stopping time
A stopping time $\tau$ is said to be bounded if there exists a constant $M$ such that $\tau < M$ almost surely.

## Step 3: Compute the expectation using the properties of Brownian Motion
Since $W_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t)$ for any $t$, we have:
$\E(W_\tau) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x f(x) dx$
where $f(x)$ is the probability density function of a normal distribution with mean 0 and variance $\tau$.

## Step 4: Simplify the integral
Since the mean of the normal distribution is 0, we have:
$\E(W_\tau) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\tau}} e^{-\frac{x^2}{2\tau}} dx$

## Step 5: Evaluate the integral
Using integration by parts, we get:
$\E(W_\tau) = 0$

The final answer is: $\boxed{0}$

Question 2:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$.

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the definition of a Brownian Motion
A Brownian motion $W_t$ has the following properties:
- $W_0 = 0$
- $W_t - W_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t-s)$ for $t > s$
- The increments are independent

## Step 2: Recall the definition of a bounded stopping time
A bounded stopping time $\tau$ satisfies $\E[\tau] < \infty$

## Step 3: Compute the expectation using the properties of Brownian Motion
Using the property that $W_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t)$ for all $t$, we have:
$\E[W_\tau^2] = \int_0^\infty \E[W_\tau^2 | \tau=t] f(t) dt$
where $f(t)$ is the density of $\tau$.

## Step 4: Simplify the expression using the properties of Brownian Motion
Since $W_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t)$ for all $t$, we have:
$\E[W_\tau^2 | \tau=t] = t$
Therefore,
$\E[W_\tau^2] = \int_0^\infty tf(t) dt$

## Step 5: Use the definition of a bounded stopping time to simplify the expression
Since $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, we have:
$\E[\tau] < \infty$
This implies that $f(t)$ has a finite support.

## Step 6: Simplify the expression using the properties of expectation
Using the linearity of expectation and the fact that $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, we have:
$\E[W_\tau^2] = \int_0^\infty tf(t) dt = \E[\tau]$

The final answer is: $\boxed{\E[\tau]}$
