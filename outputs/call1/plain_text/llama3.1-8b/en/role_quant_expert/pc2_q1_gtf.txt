Question 1:
Show that a process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if: $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t).$

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the definition of a Brownian motion
A stochastic process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if it satisfies the following properties:
- $X_0 = 0$ almost surely,
- For all $s,t \geq 0$, $\mathbb{E}[X_s] = 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = 0$,
- For all $s,t \geq 0$, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t) = \min(s,t)$,
- The increments of the process are independent, i.e., for any $t_1 < t_2 < ... < t_n$, the random variables $X_{t_1}, X_{t_2} - X_{t_1}, ..., X_{t_n} - X_{t_{n-1}}$ are independent.

## Step 2: Show that if $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion, then it satisfies the given conditions
If $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion, then:
- $X_0 = 0$ by definition,
- For all $s,t \geq 0$, $\mathbb{E}[X_s] = 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = 0$ by definition,
- For all $s,t \geq 0$, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t) = \min(s,t)$ by definition.

## Step 3: Show that if $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ satisfies the given conditions, then it is a Brownian motion
If $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ satisfies the given conditions:
- $X_0 = 0$,
- For all $s,t \geq 0$, $\mathbb{E}[X_s] = 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = 0$,
- For all $s,t \geq 0$, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t) = \min(s,t)$,
then it is a Brownian motion.

## Step 4: Show that the process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is centered Gaussian
Since for any $s,t \geq 0$, $\mathbb{E}[X_s] = 0$ and $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = 0$, and ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t) = \min(s,t)$, the process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is centered Gaussian.

The final answer is: $\boxed{1}$

Question 2:
Show that if $W_t$ is a Brownian motion, $ \{X_t=t\;W_{\frac 1t}, t>0, X_0=0 \}$ is a Brownian motion too.

Answer:
## Step 1: Verify the initial condition
We start by verifying that $X_0 = 0$. Since $W_t$ is a Brownian motion, we have $W_0 = 0$, and thus $X_0 = \frac{1}{t} W_{\frac{1}{t}} \big|_{t=0} = 0$.

## Step 2: Show that the process is centered Gaussian
Since $W_t$ is a Brownian motion, its increments are Gaussian. Therefore, for any $s < t$, we have that $\frac{1}{t} W_{\frac{1}{t}} - \frac{1}{s} W_{\frac{1}{s}} = \frac{s-t}{st} W_{\frac{1}{t}} + \frac{t-s}{st} W_{\frac{1}{s}}$ is also Gaussian. Since the process $X_t$ is a linear combination of independent Gaussian random variables, it is itself Gaussian.

## Step 3: Compute the covariance
We now compute the covariance of $X_s$ and $X_t$. We have:
$\mathrm{Cov}(X_s,X_t) = \mathrm{Cov}\left(\frac{s}{t} W_{\frac{1}{s}}, \frac{t}{s} W_{\frac{1}{t}}\right)$
$= \frac{s^2}{st} \mathrm{Cov}\left(W_{\frac{1}{s}}, W_{\frac{1}{t}}\right) + \frac{t^2}{st} \mathrm{Cov}\left(W_{\frac{1}{t}}, W_{\frac{1}{s}}\right)$
$= \frac{s^2}{st} \min\left(\frac{1}{s}, \frac{1}{t}\right) + \frac{t^2}{st} \min\left(\frac{1}{t}, \frac{1}{s}\right)$
$= s \min(1, t/s) + t \min(1, s/t)$
$= s \wedge t$

## Step 4: Verify the Brownian motion properties
We have shown that $X_t$ is a centered Gaussian process with covariance function $\mathrm{Cov}(X_s,X_t) = \inf(s,t)$. We now verify the remaining properties of a Brownian motion.

## Step 5: Stationarity and independence of increments
Since $W_t$ is a Brownian motion, its increments are stationary and independent. Therefore, for any $s < t$, we have that $\frac{1}{t} W_{\frac{1}{t}} - \frac{1}{s} W_{\frac{1}{s}} = \frac{s-t}{st} W_{\frac{1}{t}} + \frac{t-s}{st} W_{\frac{1}{s}}$ is also stationary and independent.

## Step 6: Continuity of paths
Since $W_t$ is a Brownian motion, its paths are continuous. Therefore, the paths of $X_t = t W_{\frac{1}{t}}$ are also continuous.

The final answer is: $\boxed{1}$
