Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the definition of a Brownian Motion
A Brownian Motion $W_t$ satisfies the following properties:
- $W_0 = 0$
- $W_t - W_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t-s)$ for $t > s$
- The increments are independent

## Step 2: Use the linearity of expectation
$\E(W_\tau) = \E(\E(W_\tau | \mathcal{F}_\tau))$

## Step 3: Apply the law of iterated expectations
Since $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, we can write:
$\E(W_\tau) = \int_{0}^{\infty} \E(W_t | \tau=t) f(t) dt$
where $f(t)$ is the density function of $\tau$

## Step 4: Evaluate the conditional expectation
Using the properties of Brownian Motion, we have:
$\E(W_t | \tau=t) = t \cdot \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}$

## Step 5: Compute the integral
$\E(W_\tau) = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} f(t) dt = 0$

The final answer is: $\boxed{0}$

Question 2:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$.

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the definition of Brownian Motion
A Brownian motion $W_t$ has the following properties:
- $W_0 = 0$
- $W_t - W_s \sim N(0, t-s)$ for $t > s$
- The increments are independent.

## Step 2: Use the property of Brownian Motion to compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$
Since $W_t$ is a martingale, we have that $\E(W_\tau) = W_0 = 0$. However, for Question 2, we need to compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$.

## Step 3: Apply the property of Brownian Motion to find $\E(W_\tau^2)$
Using the properties of Brownian motion and the fact that $W_t$ is a martingale, we can write:
$\E(W_\tau^2) = \E((\tau W_1)^2) = \tau^2 \E(W_1^2)$.

## Step 4: Use the definition of variance to find $\E(W_1^2)$
Since $W_t$ is a Brownian motion, we have that:
$\Var(W_1) = \E(W_1^2) - (\E(W_1))^2 = 1$

## Step 5: Solve for $\E(W_\tau^2)$
Using the result from step 4 and the fact that $W_t$ is a martingale, we have:
$\E(W_\tau^2) = \tau^2 \cdot 1 = \tau^2$

The final answer is: $\boxed{\tau^2}$
