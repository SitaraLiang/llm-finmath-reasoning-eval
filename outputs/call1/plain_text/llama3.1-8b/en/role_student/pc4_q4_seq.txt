Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the definition of a Brownian Motion
A Brownian Motion $W_t$ satisfies the following properties:
- $W_0 = 0$
- $W_t - W_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0, t-s)$ for $t > s$
- The increments are independent

## Step 2: Use the linearity of expectation
$\E(W_\tau) = \E(\E(W_\tau | \mathcal{F}_\tau))$

## Step 3: Apply the law of iterated expectations
Since $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, we can write:
$\E(W_\tau) = \int_{0}^{\infty} \E(W_t | \tau=t) d\P(t)$

## Step 4: Use the properties of Brownian Motion
For $t > s$, we have $\E(W_t - W_s | \mathcal{F}_s) = 0$.
Therefore, for a bounded stopping time $\tau$, we can write:
$\E(W_\tau) = \int_{0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}} \cdot 0 d\P(t)$

## Step 5: Evaluate the integral
Since the integrand is zero, the value of the integral does not matter.

The final answer is: $\boxed{0}$

Question 2:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$.

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the properties of Brownian motion
The Brownian motion $W_t$ has the property that for any $t \geq 0$, $\E[W_t] = 0$ and $\E[W_t^2] = t$.

## Step 2: Apply the Optional Stopping Theorem
Since $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, we can apply the Optional Stopping Theorem. This theorem states that for a bounded stopping time $\tau$, $\E[W_\tau] = \E[\lim_{t\to\infty} W_t]$ and $\E[W_\tau^2] = \E[\lim_{t\to\infty} W_t^2]$.

## Step 3: Compute the expectation of $W_\tau^2$
Using the property of Brownian motion, we have that $\E[W_\tau^2] = \E[\tau]$.

The final answer is: $\boxed{\tau}$
