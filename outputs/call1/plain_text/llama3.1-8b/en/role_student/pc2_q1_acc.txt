Question 1:
Show that a process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if: $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t).$

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the definition of Brownian motion
A stochastic process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if it satisfies the following properties:
- $X_0 = 0$
- For any $s, t \geq 0$, $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = 0$ and $\text{Cov}(X_s,X_t) = \min(s,t)$
- The process has continuous paths

## Step 2: Prove the "if" part
Assume that $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ satisfies the given conditions:
- $X_0=0$
- $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process
- $\text{Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t)$

We need to show that this implies the properties of Brownian motion.

## Step 3: Show that the process has continuous paths
Since $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, it suffices to show that $\mathbb{E}[|X_s - X_t|^2] = |s-t|$ for all $s,t \geq 0$. This follows from the fact that $\text{Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t)$ and the properties of covariance.

## Step 4: Prove the "only if" part
Assume that $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion. Then:
- $X_0 = 0$ by definition
- Since $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is Gaussian, it suffices to show that $\text{Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t)$ for all $s,t \geq 0$. This follows from the properties of Brownian motion.

## Step 5: Write down the final answer
$\boxed{\text{The process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if } X_0=0, (X_t)_{t\in \R^+} \text{ is a centered Gaussian process, and } \text{Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t).}$

Question 2:
Show that if $W_t$ is a Brownian motion, $ \{X_t=t\;W_{\frac 1t}, t>0, X_0=0 \}$ is a Brownian motion too.

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the definition of a Brownian motion
A process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if it satisfies the following properties:
- $X_0 = 0$
- $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process
- ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t) = \inf(s,t)$ for all $s, t \geq 0$

## Step 2: Verify the first property of Brownian motion
We are given that $\{X_t=t\;W_{\frac 1t}, t>0, X_0=0 \}$. Since we have $X_0 = 0$, this satisfies the first property.

## Step 3: Verify the second property of Brownian motion
To show that $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, note that for any fixed time $t > 0$, $X_t$ is a linear combination of normally distributed random variables (since it's a multiple of a Brownian motion), and hence is normally distributed. Since the mean of $W_{\frac{1}{t}}$ is zero, the mean of $X_t = t \cdot W_{\frac 1t}$ is also zero.

## Step 4: Verify the third property of Brownian motion
We need to show that ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t) = \inf(s,t)$ for all $s, t > 0$. We have:
\[{\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t) = {\rm Cov}\left(tW_{\frac{1}{t}}, sW_{\frac{1}{s}}\right)\]
Since the covariance of two independent Gaussian random variables is zero, and since $W_{\frac 1t}$ and $W_{\frac 1s}$ are independent (because they correspond to different times), we have:
\[{\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t) = {\rm Cov}\left(tW_{\frac{1}{t}}, sW_{\frac{1}{s}}\right) = ts \cdot {\rm Cov}\left(W_{\frac 1t}, W_{\frac 1s} \right)\]
Since $W$ is a Brownian motion, we have:
\[{\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t) = ts \cdot \inf\left(\frac{1}{t}, \frac{1}{s}\right) = \inf(s,t)\]

The final answer is: $\boxed{1}$
