Question 1:
Show that a process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if: $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t).$

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the definition of Brownian motion
A stochastic process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if it satisfies the following properties:
- $X_0 = 0$
- For any $s, t \geq 0$, $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = 0$ and ${\rm Var}(X_t) = t$
- The increments are independent: for any $0 \leq s < t < u$, $X_u - X_t$ is independent of $X_s$
- For any $s, t \geq 0$, $\mathbb{E}[(X_t - X_s)^2] = |t-s|$

## Step 2: Show that if $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion, then it satisfies the given conditions
If $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion, then:
- $X_0 = 0$ by definition
- For any $s, t \geq 0$, $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = 0$ and ${\rm Var}(X_t) = t$
- The increments are independent: for any $0 \leq s < t < u$, $X_u - X_t$ is independent of $X_s$

## Step 3: Show that if $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ satisfies the given conditions, then it is a Brownian motion
If $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ satisfies:
- $X_0 = 0$
- For any $s, t \geq 0$, $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = 0$ and ${\rm Var}(X_t) = t$
- The increments are independent: for any $0 \leq s < t < u$, $X_u - X_t$ is independent of $X_s$

## Step 4: Show that the covariance function satisfies the given condition
If $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion, then:
${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\mathbb{E}[(X_s - \mathbb{E}[X_s])(X_t - \mathbb{E}[X_t])]=\mathbb{E}[(X_s)(X_t)]=\inf(s,t)$

## Step 5: Show that the process is a centered Gaussian process
Since $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ has zero mean and finite variance, it is a centered Gaussian process.

Question 2:
Show that if $W_t$ is a Brownian motion, $ \{X_t=t\;W_{\frac 1t}, t>0, X_0=0 \}$ is a Brownian motion too.

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the definition of a Brownian motion
A stochastic process $\{W_t, t \geq 0\}$ is a Brownian motion if it satisfies the following properties:
- $W_0 = 0$
- $W_t$ has continuous paths for all $t \geq 0$
- $E[W_t] = 0$ for all $t \geq 0$
- $Var(W_t) = t$ for all $t \geq 0$
- For any $s, t \geq 0$, the increments $W_s$ and $W_t - W_s$ are independent.

## Step 2: Verify that $\{X_t=t\;W_{\frac 1t}, t>0, X_0=0 \}$ satisfies the first property of a Brownian motion
Since $W_0 = 0$, we have $X_0 = 0$ as well.

## Step 3: Verify that $\{X_t=t\;W_{\frac 1t}, t>0, X_0=0 \}$ satisfies the second property of a Brownian motion
Since $W_t$ has continuous paths for all $t \geq 0$, and $t W_{\frac 1t}$ is also continuous in $t$, we have that $\{X_t=t\;W_{\frac 1t}, t>0, X_0=0 \}$ satisfies the second property.

## Step 4: Verify that $\{X_t=t\;W_{\frac 1t}, t>0, X_0=0 \}$ satisfies the third property of a Brownian motion
We have $E[X_t] = E[t W_{\frac 1t}] = t E[W_{\frac 1t}] = 0$.

## Step 5: Verify that $\{X_t=t\;W_{\frac 1t}, t>0, X_0=0 \}$ satisfies the fourth property of a Brownian motion
We have $Var(X_t) = Var(t W_{\frac 1t}) = t^2 Var(W_{\frac 1t}) = t$.

## Step 6: Verify that $\{X_t=t\;W_{\frac 1t}, t>0, X_0=0 \}$ satisfies the fifth property of a Brownian motion
For any $s, t > 0$, we have that $X_s$ and $X_t - X_s$ are independent since they are functions of independent increments of $W_{\frac 1t}$.

The final answer is: $\boxed{1}$
