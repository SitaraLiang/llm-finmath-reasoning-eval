Question 1:
Show that a process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if: $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t).$

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the definition of Brownian motion
A stochastic process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if it satisfies the following properties:
- $X_0 = 0$
- For any $s, t \geq 0$, $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = 0$ and $\text{Var}(X_t) = t$
- The increments are independent: for any $0 \leq s < t < u$, $X_u - X_t$ is independent of $X_s - X_0$
- For any $s, t \geq 0$, the increment $X_{t+s} - X_t$ has the same distribution as $X_s$

## Step 2: Show that if $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion, then it satisfies the given conditions
If $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion, then:
- By definition, $X_0 = 0$
- Since $\mathbb{E}[X_t] = 0$ and $\text{Var}(X_t) = t$, the process is centered Gaussian
- The covariance function is given by ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t)$, which follows from the fact that for $s < t$, $\mathbb{E}[(X_t - X_s)(X_t + X_s)] = \text{Var}(X_t) = t$ and for $s > t$, $\mathbb{E}[(X_t - X_s)(X_t + X_s)] = 0$

## Step 3: Show that if the process satisfies the given conditions, then it is a Brownian motion
Conversely, suppose $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ satisfies:
- $X_0 = 0$
- The process is centered Gaussian
- ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t)$

## Step 4: Derive the distribution of increments from the covariance function
From the covariance function, we have that for any $s < t$, $\mathbb{E}[(X_t - X_s)(X_t + X_s)] = \text{Var}(X_t) = t$. This implies that $X_t - X_s$ is independent of $X_s$ and has variance $t-s$.

## Step 5: Show that the increments are normally distributed
Since $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is centered Gaussian, any linear combination of its components is also normally distributed. In particular, for any $s < t$, $X_t - X_s$ is normally distributed with mean 0 and variance $t-s$.

## Step 6: Show that the increments have the same distribution as a Brownian motion
For any $s < t < u$, we can write $X_u - X_t = (X_u - X_{t+s}) + (X_{t+s} - X_s)$. Since $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is centered Gaussian, the increments are independent. Moreover, since $\mathbb{E}[X_u - X_{t+s}] = 0$ and $\text{Var}(X_u - X_{t+s}) = u-t-s$, we have that $X_u - X_t$ has the same distribution as a Brownian motion.

## Step 7: Conclude that $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion
Since $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ satisfies all the properties of a Brownian motion, we conclude that it is indeed a Brownian motion.
