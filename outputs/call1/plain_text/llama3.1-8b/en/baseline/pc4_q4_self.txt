Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the definition of Brownian Motion
A Brownian motion $W_t$ has the property that for any $t \geq 0$, $\E[W_t] = 0$.

## Step 2: Apply the property to a bounded stopping time
Since $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, we can consider it as a random variable with values in $[0,T]$ for some fixed $T$. Then, by linearity of expectation and the fact that $\E[W_t] = 0$, we have:

$$\E[W_\tau] = \E[\E[W_\tau | \mathcal{F}_\tau]]$$

## Step 3: Use the law of iterated expectations
By the law of iterated expectations, we can write:

$$\E[W_\tau] = \E[\E[W_\tau | \mathcal{F}_\tau]] = \E[0]$$

The final answer is: $\boxed{0}$

Question 2:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$.

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the definition of Brownian Motion
A Brownian motion $W_t$ has the property that for any $t \geq 0$, $\E[W_t] = 0$ and $\E[W_t^2] = t$.

## Step 2: Apply the property to a bounded stopping time
Since $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, we can consider it as a random variable with values in $[0,T]$ for some fixed $T$. Then, by linearity of expectation and the fact that $\E[W_t^2] = t$, we have:

$$\E[W_\tau^2] = \E[\tau]$$

## Step 3: Use the properties of Brownian Motion
Since $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, it has finite expectation. Therefore, we can write:

$$\E[W_\tau^2] = \E[\tau]$$

The final answer is: $\boxed{\E[\tau]}$
