Question 1:
Show that a process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if: $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t).$

Answer:
## Step 1: Recall the definition of Brownian motion
A stochastic process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if it satisfies the following properties:
- $X_0=0$
- For any $s<t$, $X_t-X_s$ is independent of $\{X_u, u\leq s\}$
- For any $s<t$, $X_t-X_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0,t-s)$
- The process has continuous paths

## Step 2: Show that the given conditions imply Brownian motion
Given:
- $X_0=0$
- $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process
- ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t)$

We need to show that this implies the properties of a Brownian motion.

## Step 3: Show that the covariance function implies independence and normality
Since $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is centered Gaussian, it suffices to show that for any $s<t$, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=0$. But this follows from the given condition:
${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t)=s$ if $s<t$ and ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=t$ if $t<s$
Therefore, for any $s<t$, we have
${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=s=t-\epsilon \rightarrow 0$ as $\epsilon \rightarrow 0$

## Step 4: Show that the covariance function implies normality
Since $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is centered Gaussian, it suffices to show that for any $s<t$, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=t-s$. But this follows from the given condition:
${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t)$

## Step 5: Show that the process has continuous paths
Since $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Gaussian process, it suffices to show that for any $s<t$, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)$ is continuous. But this follows from the given condition:
${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t)$

## Step 6: Show that the conditions are necessary
Conversely, suppose $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion. Then it satisfies the properties of a Brownian motion.
- $X_0=0$ by definition
- For any $s<t$, $X_t-X_s$ is independent of $\{X_u, u\leq s\}$
- For any $s<t$, $X_t-X_s \sim \mathcal{N}(0,t-s)$
- The process has continuous paths

## Step 7: Show that the covariance function implies the conditions
Since $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion, it suffices to show that for any $s<t$, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=t-s$. But this follows from the definition of Brownian motion:
${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)={\rm Var}(X_t-X_s)$

## Step 8: Show that the conditions are equivalent
Therefore, we have shown that $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is centered Gaussian, and ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t)$.

The final answer is: $\boxed{1}$

## Step 6: Revisit the definition of a Brownian motion

## Step 7: Check if the process satisfies the third condition of a Brownian motion
