Question 1:
Show that a process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if: $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t).$

Answer:
Let $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ be a Brownian motion, and assume that $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process with covariance function ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t)$. 

Firstly, we show that $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ satisfies the Central Limit Theorem (CLT):

Assume $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process with covariance function ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t)$ and let $(Z_n)_{n\geq 1}$ be i.i.d random variables generated by the Gaussian distribution.

Then, we have:

\begin{eqnarray*}
\sqrt{n}\left(\frac{\mathbb{E}(X_{n+1}-X_n)}{X_n}-\inf(0,x)\right) \overset{d}{=} n^{-1/2} \sum_{k=1}^\infty \int_{(s-1/2)^2}^{(s+1/2)^2} Z_k^2 \,dZ_k
\end{eqnarray*}

Substituting this into the CLT, we get:

$$\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\mu}-\mu) = n^{-1/2}\sum_{k=1}^\infty (\mathbb{E}(Z_k) - Z_k)^2\Big|_{(s-1/2)^2}^{(s+1/2)^2}$$

where $\widehat{\mu}$ and $Z_k$ are the empirical mean and sample median of $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$, respectively. The above expression can be simplified as:

$$\sqrt{n}(\widehat{\mu}-\mu) = n^{-1/2}\sum_{k=1}^\infty (\mathbb{E}(Z_k^2))^{-1/2} (\mathbb{E}(Z_k)^2)^{1/2}$$

But by assumption, the covariance function satisfies: ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t)$ for all $s\in \R^+$ and $t\in \R^+$. Therefore, the covariance function of $(Z_n)_{n\geq 1}$ is equal to the covariance function of a centered Gaussian process with variance $1/2$. Thus, it follows that the sample median satisfies:

$$(\widehat{\mu}-\mu) = n^{-1/2}\sum_{k=1}^\infty (\mathbb{E}(Z_k^2))^{-1/2} (\mathbb{E}(Z_k)^2)^{1/2}$$

Therefore, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ satisfies the CLT and is a Brownian motion.
