Question 1:
Show that a process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if: $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t).$

Answer:
To solve Question 1, it suffices to show that the following conditions imply the Brownian motion assumption:
1. $X_0 = 0$ - This condition is immediately satisfied because there is no initial state for a Brownian motion.
2. $(X_t)_{t \in \R^+}$ is centered Gaussian process - The centered Gaussian process has mean zero and variance one. By definition, ${\rm Cov}(X_s, X_t) = {\rm Cov}(X_s, 0 + X_t) = (1/2){\rm Var}(X_t)$ for all $s < t$.
3. $X_0$ follows Gaussian process - This is because the Brownian motion has a covariance matrix that is symmetric and non-negative definite.
Therefore, Question 1 is answered as true and the process $(X_t)_{t \in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion.

Question 2:
Show that if $W_t$ is a Brownian motion, $ \{X_t=t\;W_{\frac 1t}, t>0, X_0=0 \}$ is a Brownian motion too.

To solve Question 2, we will use the concept of stochastic integrals (also known as the Itô formula). This formula allows us to write the differential equation for $X_t=t\;W_1$ in terms of a stochastic process, namely $W_1$, and a deterministic time parameter, denoted by $\tau$.

Let's begin by writing the differential equation for $X_t$:

$$\begin{aligned}dx_t & = v_t dt + \sqrt{2}\sigma dW_1 \\[4pt] & = v_t dt + \sqrt{2}d\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}\sin(t-s)ds+\int_{\tau}^t \cos(s-t)ds\right) \\[8pt] & = v_t d\tau + \sqrt{2} \sigma dW_{\frac1{\tau}}
\end{aligned}$$

Now, let's substitute this expression into the stochastic integral equation for $X$ with the Brownian motion:

$$dX_{t}=v_{t}dt+\sqrt{2}\sigma d\left(\int_0^\infty e^{-\frac1{\tau^2}(t-s)}ds\right) + \sqrt{2}\sigma dW_{\frac1{\tau}}$$

By the Itô formula, we have:

$$dX_{t}=v_{t}dt+\sqrt{2}\sigma dW_1 + \sqrt{2}\sigma d(X_t-t\int_0^1 e^{-\frac1{t^2}(s-u)}du)$$

Thus, the equation for $X$ can be written in terms of a Brownian motion and a deterministic time parameter $\tau$:

$$dX_{\tau}=v_{\tau}dt+\sqrt{2}\sigma d\left(\int_0^\infty e^{-\frac1{\tau^2}(t-s)}ds\right) + \sqrt{2}\sigma d(X_\tau - \tau\int_0^\tau e^{-\frac1{t^2}(s-\frac1{t})}dW_1)$$

Since $X_t=t\;W_{\frac 1t}$, this is the same equation as before. Therefore, $X$ is a Brownian motion.
