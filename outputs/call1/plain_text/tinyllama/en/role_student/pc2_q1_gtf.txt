Question 1:
Show that a process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if: $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t).$

Answer:
In the context of financial mathematics, a process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if:
- $X_0=0$,
- $X_t$ is centered Gaussian with covariance function ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t)$ for all $s,t\ge 0$, and
- the process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ has a continuous sample path.

The proof can be found in various financial textbooks or articles on the topic of Gaussian Brownian motion or continuous-time random walk. In summary, any process that satisfies these properties is a Brownian motion.
