Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
Let's first assume that the Brownian motion $W_t$ is independent of the filtration $\mathcal{F}$ generated by the Brownian motions on $[0,T]$ (which satisfies $(\mathcal{F}_t)_{t \in [0,T]}$) and let us define the stopping time $\tau:=(2\pi)^{-1}e^{-2\epsilon^2/(8\sigma^2)}|W_0|$. We have
\begin{align*}
\E(W_\tau) = \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-s^2/(8\sigma^2)}P_0(ds) = 1 - e^{-2\epsilon^2/(8\sigma^2)}
\end{align*}
where $P_0$ denotes the distribution of $(W_t)_{t \in [0,T]}$. Now we can prove the result: 
$$\E(W_\tau) = 1 - e^{-2\epsilon^2/(8\sigma^2)}$$
using the law of total expectation and the conditional expectation $e^{-2\epsilon^2/(8\sigma^2)}|W_0|$ as a random variable. This can be easily checked using the same approach in other financial problems or exercises.

Question 2:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$.

Answer:
Global assumptions:
- $W_t$ is a Brownian Motion

Previous questions and previous answers:
- Question 1: When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$. We have
$$\E(W_\tau^2) = \int_{-a}^a W_s^2 ds + 2\int_{-a}^a W_sa^2ds + \frac{1}{2}\int_{-a}^a W_{a-}^2da = 1 + \mathcal{O}(a^3)$$
where $a$ is a constant independent of $W$. Therefore, we can write
$$\E(W_\tau^2) = 1 + O((a^3)^2)\quad\text{as } a \to \infty.$$
- Question 2: When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$ and $\E(W_\tau^2)$. We have
$$\E(W_\tau) = \int_{-a}^a e^{-s^2/(8\sigma^2)}P_0(ds) = 1 - e^{-2\epsilon^2/(8\sigma^2)}\quad\text{and}\quad\E(W_\tau^2) = \frac{1}{4\sigma^2}\int_{-a}^a e^{-s^2/(8\sigma^2)}dsda$$
using the law of total expectation and the conditional expectation $e^{-2\epsilon^2/(8\sigma^2)}$ as random variables.
