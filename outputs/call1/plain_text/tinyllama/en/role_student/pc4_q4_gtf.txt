Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
Let us consider the following financial mathematics exercise: Given a Brownian Motion $W$, we wish to determine the expectation value of the sum $\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \tau_t W_t$ over all possible stopping times $\tau$ satisfying $\tau\in{\mathscr F}$ and where ${\mathscr F}$ is the filtration generated by $W$.

To tackle this problem, we first define the local assumption:
- $\tau\in{\mathscr F}$ for all $t\in[0,T]$ such that $W_t$ has jump at time $t$.

The main tool in this exercise is the Law of Total Expectation:
$$
\E\big(\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \tau_t W_t \big) = \int_0^T \E(\tau_t) \d W_t.
$$

We can derive an expression for the expected sum by integration by parts:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\E\big(\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \tau_t W_t \big)
&= - \int_0^T \frac{\d}{\d t}\E(\tau_t) \d W_t + \int_0^T \E(\tau_t)\d\tau \\
&= -\sum_{j=1}^{T-1} \frac{\d}{\d t} \E(\tau_j | W_0,\dots,W_j) + \sum_{j=1}^{T-1} \int_{[0,T]^2}\left( \E(\tau_j) - \E\big(\tau_j | W_0,\dots,W_j\big)\right)\d W_{t_1}\d W_{t_2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

We can simplify the integral term by using a product rule for expectation:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\int_{[0,T]^2}\left( \E(\tau_j) - \E\big(\tau_j | W_0,\dots,W_j\big)\right)\d W_{t_1}\d W_{t_2}
&= \sum_{i=1}^{T-1} \int_{[0,T]^2}\left( 1 - e^{\frac{t_2-t_1}{T}}\E(\tau_i|W_0,\dots,W_j)\right)\d W_{t_1}\d W_{t_2} \\
&= \sum_{i=1}^{T-1} (\E(e^{\frac{-t_2-t_1}{T}}\tau_i | W_0,\dots,W_j) - 1) \d W_{t_1}\d W_{t_2}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, we define the following notation for convenience:
- $E[\cdot | W_0,\dots,W_j]$ is the conditional expectation given $W_0,\dots,W_j$, and
- $H(W_t)$ is a stopping time that depends on all Brownian motions up to time $t$ except $W$.

We obtain the following formula for the expected sum:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\E\big(\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \tau_t W_t \big)
&= - \int_0^T \frac{\d}{\d t}\E(\tau_t | H(W)) \d H(W) + \int_0^T \E\big(\tau_t | H(W)\big)\d\tau \\
&= -\sum_{j=1}^{T-1} \frac{\d}{\d t} \E(\tau_j|H(W)) + \int_{[0,T]^2}\left( 1 - e^{\frac{t-h}{T}}\E(\tau_j | H(W))\right)\d W_{h+1}\d W_{h}.
\end{aligned}
$$

By using the law of total expectation once again:
$$
\begin{aligned}

Question 2:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$.

Answer:
Based on the global assumptions and the previous question, we can directly apply Doob's optional stopping theorem to derive the following result:

Let $W_t$ be a Brownian motion, $\tau$ be a bounded stopping time, and $t\geq 0$. Then:

-    Precondition: $(W_t)_{t\ge 0}$ is a martingale. -    Argument: Applying Doob's optional stopping theorem to $(W_t)_{t\ge 0}$ at $\tau$ yields:

    $$\mathbb{E}[W_\tau^2] = \E[W_{\tau}]^2 + \int_{\tau}^T \E[W_s^2|\F_{s-}]ds$$
    
    Substituting the expression for $W_\tau$ and using the continuous-time case of Doob's optional stopping theorem, we obtain:
    
    $$\mathbb{E}[W_\tau^2] = 0 + \int_0^T \E[W_t^2|F_{t-}]dt$$
    
    Therefore, $\E(W_\tau^2)$ is zero for all bounded stopping times $\tau$.
