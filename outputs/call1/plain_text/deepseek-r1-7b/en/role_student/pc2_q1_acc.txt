A process \( (X_t)_{t \geq 0} \) is a Brownian motion if and only if:

1. \( X_0 = 0 \),
2. \( (X_t) \) is a centered Gaussian process,
3. The covariance function satisfies \( \text{Cov}(X_s, X_t) = \inf(s, t) \).

**Proof:**

**(⇒)** Assume \( (X_t) \) is a Brownian motion.

- **Condition 1:** By definition, \( X_0 = 0 \).
  
- **Condition 2:** Each \( X_t \) follows a normal distribution with mean 0 and variance \( t \), hence it's a centered Gaussian process.
  
- **Condition 3:** For \( s \leq t \), the covariance is \( \text{Cov}(X_s, X_t) = \text{Var}(X_s) = s \). Similarly, for \( t < s \), it's \( t \). Thus, \( \text{Cov}(X_s, X_t) = \inf(s, t) \).

**(⇐)** Assume the three conditions hold.

- **Condition 1:** \( X_0 = 0 \).
  
- **Condition 2:** Each \( X_t \) is Gaussian with mean 0.
  
- **Condition 3:** The covariance structure implies independent increments. For disjoint intervals, the covariance between increments is zero, ensuring independence. Additionally, each increment has variance equal to its time interval.

Thus, \( (X_t) \) satisfies all properties of a Brownian motion: it starts at zero, has independent and stationary Gaussian increments with variance equal to time. Therefore, \( (X_t) \) is a Brownian motion.
