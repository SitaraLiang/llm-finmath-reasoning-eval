- By definition, \( X_0 = 0 \).
- Each \( X_t \) is normally distributed with mean zero and variance \( t \), making it a centered Gaussian process.
- The covariance between \( X_s \) and \( X_t \) is \( \min(s, t) \).

**(⇐)** Assume the three conditions hold. Then:

- Starting at zero ensures the initial condition of Brownian motion.
- Being a centered Gaussian process implies all finite-dimensional distributions are multivariate normal with mean zero.
- The covariance structure matches that of Brownian motion, ensuring independent increments with variance \( t - s \) for \( t > s \).

Thus, both directions confirm the equivalence.
