A process \( (X_t)_{t \geq 0} \) is a Brownian motion if and only if it satisfies the following:

1. **Starts at zero**: \( X_0 = 0 \).
2. **Centered Gaussian process**: For each \( t \geq 0 \), \( X_t \) is normally distributed with mean 0.
3. **Covariance structure**: The covariance between \( X_s \) and \( X_t \) is \( \min(s, t) \).

**Proof:**

**(⇒)** Assume \( (X_t) \) is a Brownian motion.

- By definition, \( X_0 = 0 \).
- Each increment \( X_t - X_s \) for \( t > s \) is normally distributed with mean 0 and variance \( t - s \), hence \( X_t \) is Gaussian.
- The covariance \( \text{Cov}(X_s, X_t) = \min(s, t) \).

**(⇐)** Assume the three conditions hold.

- Since \( (X_t) \) is a centered Gaussian process and has covariance \( \min(s, t) \), it satisfies the properties of Brownian motion: independent increments with variance proportional to time.
- Therefore, \( (X_t) \) must be a Brownian motion.

**Answer:**  
A process \( (X_t)_{t\geq 0} \) is a Brownian motion if and only if \( X_0 = 0 \), it's a centered Gaussian process, and its covariance satisfies \( \text{Cov}(X_s, X_t) = \min(s, t) \).
