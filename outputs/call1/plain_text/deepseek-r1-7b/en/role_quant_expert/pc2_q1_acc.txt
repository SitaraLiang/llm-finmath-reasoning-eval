1. \( X_0 = 0 \),
2. \( (X_t) \) is a centered Gaussian process,
3. The covariance function satisfies \( \text{Cov}(X_s, X_t) = \min(s, t) \).

**Proof:**

**(⇒)** If \( (X_t) \) is a Brownian motion:
- \( X_0 = 0 \).
- Each \( X_t \) is Gaussian with mean 0 and variance \( t \), making it a centered Gaussian process.
- The covariance function is \( \text{Cov}(X_s, X_t) = \min(s, t) \).

**(⇐)** If \( (X_t) \) satisfies the three conditions:
- \( X_0 = 0 \).
- Each \( X_t \) has mean 0 and variance \( t \), hence is Gaussian.
- Increments are independent: For \( s < t \), \( X_t - X_s \) is normal with mean 0 and variance \( t - s \). Independence from past increments follows from zero covariance, implying independence.

Thus, all properties of a Brownian motion are satisfied.
