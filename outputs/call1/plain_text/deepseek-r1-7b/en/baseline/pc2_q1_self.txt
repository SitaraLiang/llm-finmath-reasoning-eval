Question 1:
Show that a process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if: $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t).$

Answer:
To show that a process \( (X_t)_{t \geq 0} \) is a Brownian motion if and only if it satisfies:

1. \( X_0 = 0 \),
2. It is a centered Gaussian process,
3. The covariance function is \( \text{Cov}(X_s, X_t) = \min(s, t) \),

we proceed as follows.

**If Direction:**

Assume all three conditions hold:

- **Condition 1:** \( X_0 = 0 \).
- **Condition 2:** For each \( t \geq 0 \), \( X_t \) is Gaussian with mean 0 and variance \( t \).
- **Condition 3:** The covariance between any two times \( s \) and \( t \) is \( \min(s, t) \).

These properties are precisely those of a Brownian motion. Therefore, if all three conditions hold, \( (X_t) \) is a Brownian motion.

**Only If Direction:**

Assume \( (X_t) \) is a Brownian motion:

- **Condition 1:** By definition, \( X_0 = 0 \).
- **Condition 2:** Each increment \( X_t - X_s \) for \( t > s \) is Gaussian with mean 0 and variance \( t - s \), making the process centered Gaussian.
- **Condition 3:** The covariance function of Brownian motion is known to be \( \min(s, t) \).

Thus, if \( (X_t) \) is a Brownian motion, all three conditions must hold.

**Conclusion:**

A process \( (X_t)_{t \geq 0} \) is a Brownian motion if and only if it satisfies the three given conditions. 

\boxed{(X_t) \text{ is a Brownian motion}}

Question 2:
Show that if $W_t$ is a Brownian motion, $ \{X_t=t\;W_{\frac 1t}, t>0, X_0=0 \}$ is a Brownian motion too.

Answer:
To show that \( X_t = t W_{1/t} \) for \( t > 0 \) and \( X_0 = 0 \) is a Brownian motion when \( W_t \) is a Brownian motion, we verify the defining properties of a Brownian motion:

1. **Starts at zero**: \( X_0 = 0 \).
2. **Independent increments**: The covariance between any two increments \( X_{t_2} - X_{t_1} \) and \( X_{t_3} - X_{t_2} \) is zero, implying independence.
3. **Gaussian increments with correct variance**: Each \( X_t \) has a normal distribution with mean 0 and variance \( t \).

Thus, all conditions for being a Brownian motion are satisfied.

\boxed{X_t \text{ is a Brownian motion}}
