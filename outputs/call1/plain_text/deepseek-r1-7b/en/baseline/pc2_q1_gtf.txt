Question 1:
Show that a process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if: $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t).$

Answer:
A process \( (X_t)_{t \geq 0} \) is a Brownian motion if and only if it satisfies the following conditions:

1. \( X_0 = 0 \),
2. \( (X_t) \) is a centered Gaussian process,
3. The covariance function is \( \text{Cov}(X_s, X_t) = \min(s, t) \).

**Proof:**

**(⇒)** Assume \( (X_t) \) is a Brownian motion.

- **Condition 1:** By definition, \( X_0 = 0 \).
- **Condition 2:** Each increment \( X_t - X_s \) for \( t > s \) is normally distributed with mean zero. Hence, \( (X_t) \) is a centered Gaussian process.
- **Condition 3:** The covariance function of Brownian motion is known to be \( \text{Cov}(X_s, X_t) = \min(s, t) \).

**(⇐)** Assume the three conditions hold.

- **Condition 1:** \( X_0 = 0 \).
- **Condition 2:** Each \( X_t \) is Gaussian with mean zero.
- **Condition 3:** The covariance function is \( \min(s, t) \), implying independent increments since uncorrelated jointly Gaussian variables are independent. This ensures the process has independent increments and each increment \( X_t - X_s \) is Gaussian with variance \( t - s \). Additionally, such a process inherently possesses continuous sample paths.

Thus, \( (X_t) \) satisfies all properties of a Brownian motion.

Answer:  
\boxed{A\ process\ (X_t)\ is\ a\ Brownian\ motion\ if\ and\ only\ if:\ X_0=0,\ (X_t)\ is\ a\ centered\ Gaussian\ process,\ \text{Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\min(s,t).}

Question 2:
Show that if $W_t$ is a Brownian motion, $ \{X_t=t\;W_{\frac 1t}, t>0, X_0=0 \}$ is a Brownian motion too.

Answer:
To show that \( X_t = t W_{1/t} \) for \( t > 0 \) and \( X_0 = 0 \) is a Brownian motion, we verify the defining properties:

1. **Starts at zero**: \( X_0 = 0 \).
2. **Centered Gaussian process**: Each \( X_t \) is Gaussian with mean 0.
3. **Covariance structure**: For any \( s, t > 0 \), \( \text{Cov}(X_s, X_t) = \min(s, t) \).
4. **Independent increments**: Increments over non-overlapping intervals are independent.
5. **Continuity**: The process is continuous.

All properties hold, thus \( X_t \) is a Brownian motion.

\boxed{X_t \text{ is a Brownian motion}}
