   \[
   d(M_t^2) = 2 M_t dM_t + (dM_t)^2
   \]
3. Applying this to the Brownian motion \(W_t\), we get:
   \[
   d((W_t)^2) = 2 W_t dW_t + (dW_t)^2
   \]

   \[
   (W_{\tau})^2 - (W_0)^2 = \int_0^\tau 2 W_s dW_s + \int_0^\tau (dW_s)^2
   \]

   \[
   \mathbb{E}[(W_{\tau})^2] = \mathbb{E}\left[\int_0^\tau (dW_s)^2\right]
   \]
6. Since each increment of Brownian motion has variance \(s\), we have:
   \[
   \mathbb{E}\left[\int_0^\tau (dW_s)^2\right] = \int_0^\tau s ds = \frac{\tau^2}{2}
   \]
