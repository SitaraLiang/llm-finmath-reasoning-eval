Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
To compute the expected value of \( W_\tau \) where \( \tau \) is a bounded stopping time and \( W_t \) is a Brownian Motion, we can use several key concepts from stochastic processes.

1. **Stopping Time**: A stopping time \( \tau \) is an indicator of when some event occurs and is adapted to the filtration.
2. **Bounded Stopping Time**: Since \( \tau \) is bounded, it has an upper limit, say \( T \). This means we know whether the stopping time happened by time \( T \).
3. **Brownian Motion Properties**: Brownian Motion increments are independent and normally distributed with mean zero. For a standard Brownian Motion starting at 0, each increment from \( W_{t-} \) to \( W_t \) is a normal random variable with mean 0.
4. **Stopping Process**: Stopping a Brownian Motion at time \( \tau \) results in a process where the expected value is unchanged because all increments before \( \tau \) cancel out.

Using these properties, we can conclude that:
- The stopped process \( W_\tau \) is adapted to its own filtration.
- Since each increment from \( W_{t} \) to \( W_{t+1} \) has an expected value of zero and the increments are independent, the total expectation remains zero.

Thus, the expected value of \( W_\tau \) when \( \tau \) is a bounded stopping time is:

\[
\boxed{0}
\]

Question 2:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$.

Answer:
To compute \( \E(W_{\tau}^2) \), we can utilize the properties of a martingale.

1. **Martingales and Optional Stopping Theorem**: 
   - Consider the process \( W_t^2 - t \). Its increment is \( (W_{t+1} - W_t)^2 - 1 \).
   - Since \( W_{t+1} - W_t \) is normal with mean 0 and variance 1, its square has expectation 1.
   - Thus, \( (W_{t+1}^2 - (t+1)) - (W_t^2 - t) = 0 \), making it a martingale.

2. **Application of OST**:
   - For any bounded stopping time \( \tau \), the optional stopping theorem gives \( \E[W_{\tau}^2 - \tau] = \E[W_0^2] = 0 \).
   - Therefore, \( \E[W_{\tau}^2] = \E[\tau] \).

3. **Direct Calculation**:
   - Expand \( W_{\tau}^2 \) into increments.
   - Each increment contributes an expectation of 1, leading to \( \E[W_{\tau}^2] = \tau \).

**Conclusion**: The expected value of \( W_{\tau}^2 \) is equal to the expectation of the stopping time \( \tau \), i.e., \( \E(W_{\tau}^2) = \E(\tau) \).
