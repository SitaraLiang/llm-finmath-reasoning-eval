Answer:
To show that the process $ X_t = t W_{\frac{1}{t}} $ is a Brownian motion, we proceed as follows:

---

### **1. If $ W_t $ is a Brownian motion, then $ X_t = t W_{\frac{1}{t}} $ is a Brownian motion**

- **Step 1:** Let $ W_t $ be a standard Brownian motion. Then, $ W_{\frac{1}{t}} $ is a standard Brownian motion at time $ \frac{1}{t} $.

- **Step 2:** The process $ X_t = t W_{\frac{1}{t}} $ is a scaled and shifted version of the standard Brownian motion. Specifically, $ X_t = t \cdot W_{\frac{1}{t}} $.

- **Step 3:** Consider the increment $ X_{t+1} - X_t $. Using the definition of Brownian motion, we have:

  $$
  X_{t+1} = (t+1) W_{1(t+1)}, \quad X_t = t W_{1t}.
  $$

  Therefore:

  $$
  X_{t+1} - X_t = (t+1) W_{1(t+1)} - t W_{1t}.
  $$

  Since $ W_{1(t+1)} = W_{1t} + (W_{1t+1} - W_{1t}) $, and $ W_{1t+1} - W_{1t} $ is a standard Brownian increment, this becomes:

  $$
  X_{t+1} - X_t = W_{1t} + (t+1)(W_{1t+1} - W_{1t}).
  $$

  Since $ W_{1t} $ is a standard Brownian motion, and $ W_{1t+1} - W_{1t} $ is also a standard Brownian increment, the entire expression is a standard Brownian motion.

- **Conclusion:** The process $ X_t = t W_{\frac{1}{t}} $ is a Brownian motion.
