Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
To compute the expected value of $ \mathbb{E}(W_\tau) $, where $ \tau $ is a **bounded stopping time**, we rely on the fundamental properties of Brownian motion and the martingale structure of the process.

---

### Key Properties of Brownian Motion

- $ W_t $ is a **continuous-time stochastic process** with independent and identically distributed increments.
- $ W_t $ is a **martingale**, meaning that for any stopping time $ \tau $, the process satisfies:
  $$
  \mathbb{E}(W_\tau) = W_0
  $$
  This holds regardless of the stopping time $ \tau $, as long as $ W_\tau $ is a martingale.

---

### Role of $ \tau $ as a Bounded Stopping Time

- A **bounded stopping time** $ \tau $ is a random variable such that $ \tau $ is finite in expectation.
- This does not affect the expectation $ \mathbb{E}(W_\tau) $, as the process $ W_t $ is a martingale, and its expectation at any stopping time is equal to its initial value.

---

### Conclusion

Since $ \tau $ is a bounded stopping time, and $ W_t $ is a martingale, we conclude that:

$$
\mathbb{E}(W_\tau) = W_0 = 0
$$
