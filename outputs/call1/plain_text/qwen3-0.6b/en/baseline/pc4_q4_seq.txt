Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
When $ \tau $ is a bounded stopping time, the expected value of $ W_\tau $ can be computed as follows:

1. **Brownian Motion Properties**: The process $ W_t $ is a continuous-time Gaussian process with mean 0 and variance $ \mathbb{E}[W_t^2] = t $.

2. **Stopping Time Behavior**: Since $ \tau $ is a bounded stopping time, it is finite, and the process $ W_t $ stops at $ \tau $. This implies that $ W_\tau $ is a random variable whose expectation is a constant.

3. **Expectation Calculation**: The expectation $ \mathbb{E}[W_\tau] $ can be expressed as the sum of the initial value $ \mathbb{E}[W_0] $ and the expectation of the integral $ \int_0^\tau W_t \, dt $. However, due to the properties of Gaussian processes, the expectation of the integral over a finite stopping time $ \tau $ is zero. Therefore, $ \mathbb{E}[W_\tau] = \mathbb{E}[W_0] + 0 = 0 $.

Thus, the expected value of $ W_\tau $ is:

$$
\boxed{0}
$$

Question 2:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$.

Answer:
To compute $ \E(W_\tau^2) $ when $ \tau $ is a bounded stopping time, we begin by analyzing the properties of the Brownian motion process $ W_t $.

---

### Key Properties of Brownian Motion

- $ W_t $ is a continuous-time stochastic process with independent increments.
- $ W_t = W_0 + \int_0^t \mu_s \, ds $, where $ \mu_s $ is the drift, and $ \mu_s = 0 $ for a standard Brownian motion.
- $ W_t $ is a martingale, meaning $ \E[W_t] = W_0 $ for all $ t $.
- The process $ W_t $ is a continuous-time process with $ \tau $ being a bounded stopping time.

---

### Understanding the Stopping Time

- A bounded stopping time $ \tau $ implies that $ \tau $ is finite.
- Since $ \tau $ is a finite stopping time, $ W_\tau $ is a finite random variable.
- Therefore, $ \E(W_\tau^2) $ is simply the square of the value of $ W_\tau $, which is a finite random variable.

---

### Applying the Martingale Property

- The process $ W_t $ is a martingale, so $ \E[W_t] = W_0 $ for all $ t $.
- This implies that $ \E[W_\tau^2] = \E[W_0^2] $, since $ \tau $ is a bounded stopping time and $ \tau $ is fixed.

---

### Final Answer

$$
\boxed{\E[W_0^2]}
$$
