Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
Let $W_t$ be a Brownian motion with $\tau$ being a bounded stopping time. For any $t \geq \tau$, the increment $W_{\tau + t} - W_t$ is normally distributed with mean 0 and variance $t$. 

Since $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, there exists an event $A$ such that $\{\tau > s\}$ if and only if for all $s < \tau$, $A \cap (\{\tau > s + t: t \geq 0\}) = A$. Define

$$
F_A(t) = \E(W_{\tau + t} - W_\tau|\{\tau > s\})
$$

for any $t \in [0, \tau]$, where $\tau$ is the event that $\tau > s$.

Since $W_t$ and $W_s$ are independent for $s < \tau$ and $t < \tau$, we have that $(W_{\tau + t} - W_\tau) - (W_{\tau + s} - W_s)$ is normally distributed with mean 0 and variance $t+s$. Therefore,

$$
F_A(t) = F_{A \cap (\{\tau > s + t: t \geq 0\})}(s)
$$

Therefore, for any bounded stopping time $\tau$, we have that $\E(W_\tau)$ is equal to the expectation of the Brownian motion $W$ at the boundary event:

$$
\E(W_\tau) = \int_0^\infty F_{A}(t)dt = 1,
$$

where the last equality follows from the fact that for any bounded stopping time $\tau$, we have that $F_A(t)$ is a step function equal to 1 on some interval $(s, t]$.

Question 2:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$.

Answer:
\begin{align*}
\E(W_\tau^2) &= \int_0^\tau \mathbb{E}[W_s^2] dP(s)\\
&= \int_0^\tau (\mathrm{Var}(W_s))^{\frac{1}{2}} ds P(0) + 2\int_0^\tau \mathrm{Cov}(W_s,W_\tau)ds P(0)\\
&= \left[\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{Var}(W_s)\right]_{s=0}^{\tau} P(0) + 2\int_0^\tau \mathrm{Cov}(W_s,W_\tau) ds P(0).
\end{align*}
