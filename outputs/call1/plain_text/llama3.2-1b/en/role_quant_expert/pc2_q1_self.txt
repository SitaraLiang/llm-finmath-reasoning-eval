Question 1:
Show that a process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if: $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t).$

Answer:
Let's denote $Y_t = X_t - X_{t-}$. Then:
$$
\begin{aligned}
Y_0 &= X_0 - X_{0-}\\
&= X_0\\
\mathbb{E}[Y_t] &= \mathbb{E}[(X_t - X_{t-})]\\
&= \int_{t-}^t \mathbb{Cov}(X_u,X_v) du + \mathbb{E}[X_t]\\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\mathbb{E}[Y_1] = Y_0 = X_0 - X_{0-}$ and $Y_0 = 0$, it follows that:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}[Y_1] &= \mathbb{E}[(X_1-X_0)]=0\\
&= \int_0^1 \mathbb{Cov}(X_u,X_1) du + \mathbb{E}[X_1]\\
&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence $Y_t$ is a martingale. By uniqueness of the Girsanov process, $X_t = X_{t-} + Y_t$ is a Brownian motion if and only if:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb{E}[X_t] &= \int_0^t \mathbb{Cov}(X_u,X_v) dv + \sigma^2 t\\
&= \frac{1}{2}\left(t^2+3t\right)
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\mathbb{Cov}(Y_s,Y_t)=\inf(s,t)$, we conclude that $X_0=0$ and $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process.

Question 2:
