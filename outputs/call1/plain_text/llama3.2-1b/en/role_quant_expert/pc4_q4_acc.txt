Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
$\E(W_\tau) = \int_{0}^{\tau} \mathbb{P}(W_s > w) dw$

This can be rewritten as:

$\E(W_\tau) = \int_{0}^{\tau} (1 - \mathbb{P}(W_s > w)) dw$

Question 2:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$.

Answer:
\begin{align*}
\E(W_\tau^2) &= \E(\left(\sum_{i=1}^\infty (W_t - W_i)_+\right)^2) \\
&= \E\left(\sum_{i=1}^\infty \left(\sum_{j=1}^{i-1} (W_t - W_j)_+\right)\right)^2 \\
&= \sum_{k=1}^\infty \E\left(\sum_{i=k}^\infty (W_t - W_i)_+^2\right) \\
&= \sum_{k=1}^\infty \E(W_\tau-kW_0)^2.
\end{align*}

Note: $W_0$ is a constant, and $\E(W_\tau-kW_0)$ can be evaluated as the expectation of $W_\tau$ under the Brownian motion starting from zero.
