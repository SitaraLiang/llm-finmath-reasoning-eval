Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
$\E(W_\tau) = \int_0^\infty W_s dW_s$

Question 2:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$.

Answer:
$\E(W_\tau^2) = \int_0^\infty (1 - e^{-2s})\sigma^2 ds$

where $\sigma^2$ is the quadratic variation of $W_t$. 

Proof:

Since $W_t$ is a Brownian Motion, we know that $W_{\tau + h} - W_\tau = \int_0^\tau (W_s - W_\tau) dW_s + \int_{\tau}^{h} B_u dB_u$, where $B_u$ is the increment of the Brownian motion at time $u$. 

Taking expectations on both sides, we get:

$$
\E(W_{\tau+h}^2) - \E(W_\tau^2) = \int_0^\infty (W_s - W_\tau)\sigma^2 ds + \int_0^\tau B_u dB_u + \int_{\tau}^{h} (\E(B_u))^2 du
$$

Since $B_t$ is a Gaussian Process with covariance function $\Cov(B_s, B_t) = \min(s,t)$, we have:

$$
\int_0^\infty (W_s - W_\tau)\sigma^2 ds + \int_{\tau}^{h} (\E(B_u))^2 du = \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 h + \E(B_h) h
$$

Using the Optional Stopping Theorem, we can stop at $\tau$ and get:

$$
\int_0^\infty (W_s - W_\tau)\sigma^2 ds = \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 (\tau-h) + h \E(B_h)
$$

Since $B_t$ is a Gaussian Process, we know that $\E(B_h) = 0$. Therefore:

$$
\int_0^\infty (W_s - W_\tau)\sigma^2 ds = \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 (\tau-h)
$$

Substituting this back into the original equation, we get:

$$
\E(W_\tau^2) = \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 (\tau-\infty) + h \E(B_h)
$$
