Question 1:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau)$.

Answer:
$\E(W_\tau) = \frac{1}{\sigma^2} E\left[\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\tau - \mathbb{T}_0 W_\tau\right)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)\right]$

where $T_0$ is the initial time, $\sigma > 0$, and $\mathbb{T}_0 = \inf \{t \geq 0: W_t \in [W_{\mathbb{T}_0}, W_{\tau}] \text{ for some } t \geq \mathbb{T}_0\}$.

In other words, the expectation of $W_\tau$ can be computed as follows:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\E(W_\tau) &= \frac{1}{\sigma^2} E\left[\exp\left(-\frac{\left(\tau - \mathbb{T}_0 W_\tau\right)^2}{2\sigma^2}\right)\right] \\
&= \frac{1}{\sigma^2} \int_{W_0}^\infty e^{-x^2/2\sigma^2} dx
\end{aligned}
$$

Question 2:
When $\tau$ is a bounded stopping time, compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$.

Answer:
To compute $\E(W_\tau^2)$ for a Brownian motion $W_t$, we use the properties of quadratic variations and the fact that $(\cdot)^2$ is convex.


\begin{align*}
\mathbb{E}(W_\tau^2) &= \mathbb{E}((W_{\tau - 1})^2 + (W_\tau)^2 - W_{\tau-1}^2) \\
&= \mathbb{E}\left(\int_0^\tau W_s^2 ds + \int_0^\tau W_\tau^2 ds\right) \\
&= \mathbb{E}\left(\int_0^\tau (W_s - W_{\tau-1})^2 ds + 2\int_0^\tau W_s W_\tau ds\right)
\end{align*}

Using the properties of quadratic variations and the fact that $W_t$ is a martingale, we can simplify this expression.

\begin{align*}
\mathbb{E}(W_\tau^2) &= \int_0^\tau (W_s - W_{\tau-1})^2 ds + 2\int_0^\tau W_s W_\tau ds \\
&= \int_0^\tau (W_s)^2 ds
\end{align*}
