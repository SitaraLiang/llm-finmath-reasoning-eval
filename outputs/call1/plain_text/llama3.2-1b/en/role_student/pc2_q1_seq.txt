Question 1:
Show that a process $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if: $X_0=0$, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process, ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t).$

Answer:
To prove the equivalence of the processes, we need to show that one implies the other.

First, assume $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion with initial condition $X_0=0$. Then it is clear that:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)&=\inf(s,t) \\
&= X_s - X_s + X_t - X_t \\
&= 0.
\end{aligned}
$$

Next, assume $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process with covariance matrix $\sigma^2 I$, where $I$ is the identity matrix and $\sigma^2 > 0$. Then we have:

$$
{\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)&=\inf(s,t) \\
&= X_s - X_s + X_t - X_t \\
&= \sqrt{X_s^2 - (X_s)^2} + \sqrt{(X_t)^2 - (X_t)^2} \\
&= \sigma\sqrt{s-t}.
$$

Since $\sigma > 0$ and $s, t > 0$, we must have $\sigma = \inf(s,t)$.

Conversely, assume $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a centered Gaussian process with covariance matrix $\sigma^2 I$. Then it follows that:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)&=\sqrt{s-t}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $s - t > 0$, we must have $\inf(s,t) = \sqrt{s-t}$.

Therefore, $(X_t)_{t\in \R^+}$ is a Brownian motion if and only if $X_0=0$ and ${\rm Cov}(X_s,X_t)=\inf(s,t)$.

Question 2:
Show that if $W_t$ is a Brownian motion, $ \{X_t=t\;W_{\frac 1t}, t>0, X_0=0 \}$ is a Brownian motion too.

Answer:
Since $X_t = t W_{\frac 1t},$

we have that $\dfrac{dX_t}{dt} = t \dfrac{dW_{\frac 1t}}{dt}.$

Using Ito's Lemma,

$X_t - X_0 = \int_0^t \left(t \dfrac{dW_{\frac 1s}}{ds}\right) ds$

$= \int_0^t s \cdot \dfrac{dW_s}{dt} ds$

$= \int_0^t s \cdot dW_s.$

Differentiating both sides with respect to $t,$ we get:

$\dfrac{dX_t - X_0}{dt} = \int_0^t s \cdot dW_s.$

Since $s = t\frac{1}{t}$ and $\dfrac{ds}{dt}= 1$, we have that

$$
\dfrac{dX_t}{dt}=\int_0^t s\cdot dW_s=\int_0^{t\frac{1}{t}}s\cdot dW_s=t\int_0^td{s}\cdot dW_s= \sqrt{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(s \cdot dt)^2 \cdot W_t} 
$$

$= \sqrt{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}s^2dt \cdot W_t}$.

Since $X_0 = 0$, we have that $\dfrac{dX_0}{dt}=0$.
